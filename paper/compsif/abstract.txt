Bias-mitigation rewrites by large language models are usually judged only by whether a bias detector's score falls, yet a rewrite can lower that score while deleting a requirement, changing a number or reversing a negation. We ask whether a transparent gate can choose a rewrite more safely than simple selection rules. FairLens combines four bias detectors, three rewriters (rule-based, FLAN-T5 and Qwen2.5) and a Reliability Gate: rules that accept, retry, reject or defer each rewrite to a person, with reason codes, based on bias reduction, similarity, entailment, fact preservation and detector agreement. A fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-small detector reached macro-F1 0.814 on 2,652 held-out sentences but flagged about 40% of neutral WinoBias sentences. Qwen2.5 reduced bias scores the most yet lost a fact in 41.7% of its rewrites. On 204 controlled test inputs, selecting the least-biased candidate chose a damaging rewrite 96.1% of the time; the Gate never did, at the cost of deferring 23.5% of inputs and keeping the biased original for 33.8%. A learned decision tree was more accurate but chose a damaging rewrite for 13.2% of inputs. Mitigation tools should be judged on what they preserve and when they abstain, not only on how much bias they remove.
